# What the model means

The code in `src/model/` is the exact version, and `src/law.js` holds every legal figure with its source. This page keeps only what the code cannot say by itself: what the figures mean and the rules that cross files.

## The offer

- An offer is `{ id, terms, mine }` (ADR-0007), plus `cmp`, the Comparar toggle, which is a view choice and stays at the top.
- `terms` is what the employer offers and the only part ever shared: dates, duration, hours, days off, contract, pay, extra pays, vacation, cash, agreed total, overnight, housing, meals given, the km rate paid, where the job is (the village `place`, INE municipality code and province code), whether it was only an offer and never worked (`offerOnly`) (both since schema version 3, ADR-0028) and the herd (head counts of sheep, goats and cattle). `DEF_TERMS` in `src/offer.js` lists them.
- `mine` is the worker's own figures for that offer: `name`, distance and trips, car, commute, other expenses, the rent override and the IRPF choice. `DEF_MINE` lists them. A field belongs to exactly one of the two.
- Whatever the employer's terms alone decide (schedule, legal minimum, the questions in `offer.js`) takes `terms`; only the car, the other expenses, the rent and the IRPF choice read `mine`.
- `vacationAuto` (vacation set for a short contract, not by the user) sits in `terms` with `vacation`.

## Terms-only figures

- `calcTermsOnly` (`model/calc.js`) calculates an offer from `terms` alone, with `termsOnly` (`offer.js`): a neutral `mine` (no car, so no trips, km or fuel; no other expenses; no rent override; automatic IRPF) and IRPF as if nothing else was earned that year (`others` 0, no other offers). It works from `{ id, terms }`, which is how other people's offers are calculated (ADR-0007).
- The rest of the settings are kept: what housing and meals are worth, Social Security rates. They value the terms; they say nothing about the worker's life. The km rate the employer pays is in `terms` but is paid per km driven at work, so with no km it is zero.
- These figures say what the job pays, not what it is worth to any one worker, so they leave out costs that differ between workers. The Comparar tab can show them for own offers (the «Solo las condiciones» switch).

## Other shepherds' offers

- Read only when signed in with sharing on, fetched on entering the Otros pastores tab and on demand, and kept in `state.others`, in memory (ADR-0030). They are never put in `state.offers`, `localStorage`, backups or the account's own rows.
- They come through `ingestShared` (`schema.js`): defaults for missing fields, unknown ones kept at the current version and dropped from a newer one, no `mine` but the sharer's name. Calculated with `calcTermsOnly` only; the Otros pastores tab ranks them together with your own offers, on terms alone for every row (ADR-0013); Comparar has only your own.
- The read-only view reuses `resultView` with `readOnly`: no editing, negotiating, copying or payslip controls.

## The account copy

- Signed in, the account is the source of truth and `localStorage` its cache (`storage/account.js`). Changes are written after a pause, and only the offers whose `terms`, `mine` or `cmp` changed.
- The first sign-in of a user on a browser uploads the offers the account lacks and keeps both sets, merged by id; ids that are not uuids get a new one first, in the browser too. Only a failed upload asks (ADR-0009). An empty account starts like a first visit, with the templates, and its placeholder offer is never written.
- Backups made before ADR-0007 hold flat offers; the version 1 upgrader (see «Saved format») splits them by `DEF_MINE` (own figures) and puts the rest in `terms`. The browser's own storage of that time (`cj.ofertas`) is still not read.
- A browser that has synced with that user before takes the account's copy as it is: an offer missing from the account was deleted on another device, except what was changed here and not yet written: offline edits, and edits made between opening the page and the account connecting. From the start of a visit with a saved session (`trackUntilConnected`, before the bundle loads), changes go into the same unsynced record (`UNSYNCED_KEY`) as offline ones, and the account's copy keeps them: edited and new offers stay, deleted ones stay deleted, settings stay.
- While a saved session connects, the page shows the cached copy, the account button «connecting» and «Cargando tu cuenta…», never a guest's sign-in; if connecting fails, it falls back to guest with «No se pudo conectar con la cuenta.».
- The blank placeholder offer of a first visit is never written.

## What is owed

- Counted per item (salary, overnight bonus, vacation) and month by month. A surplus in one item does not cover a shortfall in another (ADR-0004).
- Cash is subtracted from what is owed, but a payroll below the minimum is still illegal, because cash pays no Social Security.
- Without a contract there is no payroll: everything paid is cash, measured against a temporary contract's minimum, and the vacation is owed.

## What happened: phases and settling (ADR-0032)

- `phaseOf(offer, today)` (`model/phase.js`): `offerOnly` if `terms.offerOnly`; `undecided` without a start date or with one after today; `finished` when the end (`endOf`) is known and before today; otherwise `underway`, so an offer with no known duration stays underway. Starting or ending today is still underway.
- `settle(offer, record, today, ctx)` (`model/settle.js`) is pure. The record is `{ months: { 'YYYY-MM': { closed, days: { 'DD': 'work' | 'off' | 'sick' } } }, payments: [{ id, date, amount, via, note? }] }`; missing parts mean empty. A libranza and a vacation day are the same mark, «off»: vacation is not a day state. `amount` is net: what actually arrived (payroll after withholdings, cash as handed). Without a start date there are no months.
- A **closed month** takes its days as listed (an unlisted day is not worked); the offer cannot change it. In an **open month** a listed day is a fact and the rest follows the offer (`facts.followingOffer` is that share of the month), and the row is flagged `estimate`. Month shares are the payslips' (`monthShares`: partial months at 1/30), so the months add up to the contract. With a start but no known end, months run from the start to the month of `today` (the month in progress is an open row, due at its end); each is one month's pay (`calc` counts one month without a duration), the first at 1/30 a day if it starts mid-month, and the vacation accrues month by month instead of falling due at the end.
- What the days change: a wage «al día» is the day rate times the paid days of the month (worked, plus the vacation taken and the holidays, which follow the offer even in a closed month); the minimum of a casual contract follows the days worked. A monthly or total wage does not shrink with rest days. Days worked above the month's due workdays are `extraDays`, valued at the minimum wage and kept apart: they are not in `owed`.
- Per month and per item (salary, overnight bonus, vacation, the vacation falling due in the last month): `agreed`, `minimum`, `due` = the greater of the two, `shortfall` = what the minimum exceeds the agreed pay (the ficha's debt), `received` and `owed` = due minus received. Nothing is netted across items or months (ADR-0004). The agreed pay covers the minimum salary first, then the overnight bonus, and it is payroll plus the agreed cash: each month's cash (`row.agreedCash`, net as handed; per day worked it follows the days worked in that month, facts of a closed month included, else it is the month's weight like the pay) enters the pool as the gross that brings it net, the figure `calc` credits (its bisection, `r.cash.grossEq`, shared out over the cash; `/ r.netFactor` without a contract, as `calc` does for that debt), so total remuneration is what is compared with the minimum, as `calc` does with `coveredByCash`. Without a contract the pay is only the cash. Cash received still reduces `owed` (a cash payment is grossed up by the same ratio, so paying exactly what was agreed leaves nothing of it owed; payroll payments by `r.netFactor`), and `belowMinimumOnPayroll` stays on the declared pay alone. Cash above what a month's minimum salary and bonus need covers a vacation shortfall, as in `calc`: that much moves from the salary item to the vacation, so the total due is the pay agreed (not with a contract absent: the whole vacation is owed whatever the cash). The vacation owed comes from the offer's terms, as in `calc`, never from the days.
- Payments, cash included, are pooled, oldest first by date, and fill the months in order (salary, overnight, vacation within each; a payment names no item, so the split by item is by convention and the total owed does not depend on it). Payments are net, so each is grossed up as `calc` credits cash (`amount / r.netFactor`) before it is compared with the gross dues; an excess over everything due is `unassigned`. Cash arrived, so it reduces `owed` (as `coveredByCash` does in `calc`), and `totals.cash` (the «en negro» money) reports it apart, net as handed. Legality is separate: `declared` is the gross equivalent of what was received on payroll (`via: 'payroll'`, the only way that is declared: a payslip paid by bank transfer; `via: 'cash'` is «En mano», en negro), and `belowMinimumOnPayroll` what the minimum exceeds it by, per item and month, because cash pays no Social Security. It is never folded into `owed`. A month with something owed carries `deadline`, `LAW.claimYears` after its due date (the offer's last day for the last month), and `lapsed` once that is before today; `totals.claimable` leaves lapsed months out.
- **The screens** (`model/record.js`, `model/accounts.js`, `model/compliance.js`, `ui/record.js`, `views/record.js`, `views/calendar.js`). Every worked job starts with its agreed «Condiciones». **Registro** (under way): the months strip (cerrado, por cerrar, en curso, por venir), a line pointing to a month that waits to be closed, «Hasta hoy» (the tiles Corresponde, Cobrado with «de ello, en mano», Falta, with the notes of what the figures leave to say), the month tapped open below (calendar, counts, what the month corresponds to, «Cerrar <mes>»), «Cobros», and the rest of the offer's analysis in one closed fold. **Cuentas** (over) puts the result first and the chores last, under the small title «Cuentas»: the yellow block «Debe el ganadero» with the tiles Corresponde, Cobrado and Falta and their notes (while months are not closed, the line that the figure is not firm and the one action «Revisar los meses»); «Lo que dejó el trabajo» (`balanceOf`: Corresponde in total, Cobrado split into nómina and en mano, the jornadas, and the days contributed and the paro from the offer; «estimado» or «según la oferta» wherever a figure still follows the offer, and the fold «Cómo sale lo que corresponde» with its lines); «¿Se cumplió la ley?» (facts only, see below); «Reclamar» (the copy icon, «Plazo más cercano», the closed fold «Plazos por mes») while Falta is above 0; «Registro del trabajo» (the months, each a tap that opens its calendar alone, a closed one to reopen it; «+ Añadir <mes>»; «Cobros»), a fold that is open only while a month is not closed or there is no cobro (`data-autofold`: the page sets it, `restoreFolds` leaves it); and the closed fold «Lo que decía la oferta» (Desglose, ¿Es legal?, Final de campaña with its simulator, Cómo se calcula; not «Para negociar»), a reference and not part of the result. «Por decidir», «solo oferta» and an offer with something missing keep the headline and the full analysis. A day on the calendar is what the record holds or else the offer's default (`defaultKinds`: the days off spread evenly over the month's span); a tap flips jornada and libre, and the first tap in a month records every day as shown (`toggleDay`), dropping the month again if every day is back to the default. Closing records every day as shown and freezes the month (`closeMonth`), only once it has ended and from its calendar or «Repasar los meses»; `reopenMonth` keeps the days and asks to confirm. «Baja» has no control: a `sick` day already in the record is shown and a tap makes it a jornada. There is no list of holidays, so festivos are not marked. Where the days marked make the work run otherwise than the offer's dates, one line near «Condiciones» says «Trabajo real: del <fecha> al <fecha> (la oferta decía <fecha>)» (derived, nothing to edit); the strip and the registro's months include the months after the offer's end that hold a day worked, and after the last month shown, once the next month has started, a quiet «+ Añadir <mes>» (`addableMonth`) opens that month's calendar with every day libre: it is not part of the job until a day is marked worked in it, and has nothing to close. The claim text says the days worked after the offer's end. The calendar compares with the offer by counts of jornadas (`monthAccounts`): the difference is shown only when they differ, valued as the month settled with the offer's own days written out against the month as it is. «Repasar los meses» has a history entry (`openReview`/`closeReview`), the strip and the calendar none.
- **Today** comes from `todayISO()` (`offer.js`, local date), the only place the page asks the date; the e2e tests fix it with `page.clock`.
- **The job as it really was** (`jobShares` in `model/settle.js`, `lastWorkedDay` in `model/record.js`, `effectiveEnd` and `phaseOf(offer, today, record)` in `model/phase.js`). The record has no dates of its own and nothing is saved for this: the real period is derived from the days marked in `mine.record.months`. A month after the offer's last one enters the job only if it holds a day marked `work` (an entry with none is ignored, as if absent); the job then goes on to the last day worked, the months between included, whose days default to libre. Each day of that period (from the day after the offer's last day to the last day worked) is paid 1/30 of a month's pay (`LAW.payrollMonthDays`, as the payslips prorate a partial month: a whole month is 1): a wage per day is the day rate times the days worked, a monthly or total wage is one thirtieth per calendar day, rest days inside the period paid. The cash, the overnight bonus, the minimum, the vacation accrued (`vacationShareOf`: every month's weight, due at the end) and the paid vacation taken and holidays of a wage per day follow the same weight, over the offer's own shares; a net total agreed scales by the days as before. In a **closed** month, days libre before the first day worked of the job's first month, or after the last day worked of its last month (a closed month after the last worked or open one is outside it entirely), lie outside the job: a monthly or total wage is prorated by the days inside (1/30 a day, `weight`), a wage per day does not change (`dayWeight`); open months follow the offer. The calendar cannot tell «libre because the contract ended» from «libre as the last rest»: trailing libre days of a closed month are treated as outside the job. `settle().job` is the real period (`from`, `to`, the offer's, `differs`). `row.calendarFrom`/`calendarTo` are the days the calendar shows (the whole month for the last one, so a day after the offer's end can be marked), `row.extension` flags a month after the offer's. The phase uses the later of the offer's end and the last day worked: `finished` only if that is before today; the start stays the offer's.
- **Totals, not months** (`standingOf`, `model/accounts.js`). The job is worked with its totals: Corresponde − Cobrado = Falta, in net, `falta` never below 0 and the same word in Registro and Cuentas. Months matter only for the days that make Corresponde (a month is closed after it has ended, from its calendar); a cobro is a free entry that belongs to no month and covers none. What was cobrado beyond Corresponde (`sobra`) is a note that real payslips differ from the simulated ones, never a debt or «de más». Under way Corresponde is the months that have ended (the current month so far and the vacation are aside, payable later); once over it is the whole job. With no cobro in the record no Falta is shown, only Corresponde and a prompt, and `belowMinimumOnPayroll` is not mentioned.
- **Net** (`standingOf`, `monthAccounts`): the owner thinks in what reaches the shepherd, so what `settle` has gross is taken times `r.netFactor`, the cash agreed is the cash as handed (its gross, `agreedCashGross`, is taken out of the items first, salary first, so the lines add up), and the cobros are as they arrived. A **net total agreed** (`cashMode: 'total'`, the pay answers' «total pactado», split by `effectiveOffer` into a payroll whose net is the simulated one and a cash that is the rest) comes out as that total with no simulated withholding left in it: the payroll gross × `netFactor` is the payroll net by construction and the cash is as handed. Agreed per day, it follows the days worked alone, payroll and cash together (`r.cash.unit` keeps `'day'`, and `settle` pays the payroll per day worked, the vacation taken being inside the total), so each day worked less in a closed month takes that day's net off, down to the legal minimum, which is compared in gross and tops up. Per month or in total it follows the month's weight. Gross is only in the claim text (`recordClaimText`), where it is what is claimed, and the text says so.
- **Deadlines** keep `settle`'s oldest-first assignment of the payments, only to date the claim: `standingOf().nearest` (the first deadline of a month with something open, nothing when nothing is missing) and `deadlines` (per month, for the closed fold «Plazos por mes»). No month is accused in the main view.
- **The law, from the record** (`model/compliance.js`, `lawFromRecord`): facts only. Weekly rest and days worked above the due workdays look at CLOSED months alone: the rest counts days marked off per period of `LAW.restPeriodWeeks` weeks from the job's start, judging only the whole periods wholly inside closed months (`rest.periods` says how many; a last period shorter than the weeks it groups over is not judged), against `LAW.weeklyRest` a week; the days worked are those of closed months against the model's own due workdays (`r.extra.due`) in the share of the job that is closed. The screen says «sobre N de M meses cerrados», and with no month closed is one line, «Cierra los meses para comprobarlo». The overnight bonus (against `totals.items.overnight.owed`) and the minimum on payroll are judged only once EVERY month is closed and at least one payroll cobro exists (`known`), else «Se comprobará al cerrar todos los meses». The minimum compares what arrived on payroll, in net, with the net equivalent of the minimum (the gross minimum of every item times `r.netFactor`), and fails only beyond a margin for withholding differences: the net equivalent less one more simulated IRPF's worth of the gross minimum (`r.pay.irpfPct`), nothing else invented. The note «La nómina declarada queda X bajo el mínimo» in the yellow block is that same figure and shows under the same condition. Not checked, and said in one line: festivos worked (no list of holidays per municipality and year) and the minimum against the real payslip.
- **Cards**: a phase pill and, for En curso and Acabada, «Debe el ganadero X · N meses sin cerrar» (`cardRecord`, in net); without cobros, «Sin cobros apuntados».
- Rows are in cents, rounded by running total so each column adds up to its total as shown (`ledger.js`).
- **Invariant** (`test/unit/settle.test.js`): with an empty record, `totals` equal what `calc` shows: agreed pay (`pay.salary` plus the agreed cash as the gross that brings it net, `r.cash.grossEq`; `pay.vacationPay` for the vacation), minimums (`min`), and the total `shortfall` equals the debt after the cash, `r.debt.owed`, to the cent. By item (`debt.parts`) it is compared only where no cash is involved, since `calc` has no item split of what the cash covers. It holds for every sample offer, with or without a contract. Without a contract the pay is only the cash, and the vacation is owed whatever the cash. Self-employed work has no due or debt (ADR-0029).
- Open choices, marked `TODO:` in the code: sick days are not paid; a holiday worked counts twice; extra days are not yet in `owed`.

## Extra time

Two rules, and each extra hour counts once.

- **Paid days:** a wage «al día» is paid for the whole days worked (`daysWorked`, rounded) plus the public holidays and vacation taken off, which are paid too (`paidDays` in `model/time.js`). With nothing taken off, pay per day worked is exactly the daily wage.
- **Extra days:** days worked above the due workdays (the contract's days minus weekly rest, public holidays and vacation taken). Each is valued as a normal workday at the minimum wage.
- **Overtime:** hours above the normal workday, on every day worked, valued at the minimum hourly wage.
- Whatever is paid above the legal minimum, on payroll and in cash, covers the extra days first and the overtime after.

## Earnings and savings

- «Se gana» (`earnings.total`, `perHour`, `perDay`, `perMonth`, and the parts in `PARTS`) is money only: what reaches the worker, plus housing and food when counted, minus car and other expenses. The unemployment benefit a contribution generates is not part of it (ADR-0022): `r.unemployment` keeps its days and euros, shown apart under the headline. The old «Contar el paro que generas» setting is gone; `ingestSaved` drops a saved `unemployment` value.
- **Paro lost to money en negro** (`r.unemployment.lost`): the benefit that the declared pay plus the gross equivalent of the cash (`r.cash.grossEq`) would give, minus the benefit generated, over the same contributed days (a month when there is no duration). Without a contract, all of it. Zero when the declared pay already reaches the benefit's maximum, and without cash. Only the euros differ when there is a contract: the days contributed are the same.
- **Comparar's ranking key** is «Se gana» plus the paro, both in the chosen unit (`inUnit`); with «Solo lo que se gana» (not saved) it is «Se gana» alone. The sum is never shown. Offers without a duration, in «en total», go last. The chart over time, the yearly summary and the year headers of Ofertas use money only.
- The law's figures (Social Security rates, overnight bonus, vacation days, annual hours) are read from `src/law.js`, not from the settings; values saved for them are dropped on load (`ingestSaved`).
- **Savings** is what the employer saves at the worker's expense, with their Social Security: the contributions on the cash part, the missing minimum, and the unpaid extra days and overtime. It is the worker's margin to negotiate.

## Agreed totals and net pay

- An agreed total is split into payroll and cash: the cash part is the total minus what reaches the worker on payroll, net, and never below zero.
- A net pay is turned into the gross that gives it, because IRPF depends on the gross. The same conversion says what gross would bring the cash part net.
- IRPF counts the worker's other worked offers in the same year. Those are calculated on their own to avoid circular references.

## Pay questions

The «Pago» section of the form is a view over `terms`; it saves no fields of its own (`src/pay-answers.js`). The first amount is in `unit` (day, month, or total when the offer has a duration).

| Answer | Fields |
|---|---|
| Todo | `payrollMin: false`, `payType: 'net'`, `payAmount` and `payUnit` = amount and unit, `cashMode: 'amount'`, `cashAmount: ''` |
| El mínimo (el salario mínimo) | `payrollMin: true`, `cashMode: 'total'`, `totalAmount` and `totalUnit` = amount and unit |
| Una parte | `payrollMin: false`, `payAmount` = the part, `payType` net or gross, `payUnit` = unit, `cashMode: 'total'`, `totalAmount` and `totalUnit` = amount and unit |
| Sin contrato | `contract: 'none'`, `cashAmount` and `cashUnit` = amount and unit |

En negro is never asked: it is the model's `split.cash`. «Una parte» without the part is missing (`payrollPart`), as the model would take all of it as en negro. The vacation answers are `terms.vacation`; «Las libro» is hidden when `vacationPayable`: the contract is casual (`isCasual`: temporary, `natDays` of the schedule up to `LAW.casualMaxDays`) or lasts a month or less of any kind (`isShortContract`), the cases where the vacation may be paid instead of taken. The same rule decides whether paying it is legal in `rights.js`, so an option the form hides is never the one the verdict asks for. It does not change the default `defaultVacation` sets (only a month or less); an offer that already has «Las libro» keeps it, with a hint.

Shapes the answers cannot say are converted once, in `ingestSaved` (every load path), by `toAnswerable`: a yearly amount becomes monthly, «total» without a duration becomes a month, and a payslip with en negro money, or a part whose unit differs from the total's, becomes «Una parte» with total = net payslip + en negro in the payslip's unit. A conversion is kept only if the earnings, gross, net, cash and debt figures stay within a cent. The exception is a net payslip with en negro money and vacation inside the pay: with a total the model puts the vacation on top of the payslip, so both amounts are kept and only that treatment of the vacation moves.

## Rules added in November 2026

- Social Security rates depend on the contract: `ssOf(contract)` in `law.js` (Orden PJC/297/2026), read through `r.pay.rates`. An offer without a contract uses the fijo discontinuo rates, the most common contract in shepherding, to turn cash into gross and to price what the employer saves (owner, 1 November 2026).
- Every ledger's «=» row is the sum of its rows as shown: rows are rounded first, then added, per column (`test/e2e/ledger.js` checks exact equality). A total can therefore differ by a cent or a euro from the same figure computed elsewhere. «Ganado» is not one of those: the headline, Comparar (ranking and head to head) and the share card show the breakdown's sum in their unit (`earnedShown` in `units.js`, with the columns' rounding `shownInColumns`), so they always match; only units the breakdown has no column for (per hour) round the exact figure. The rule lives in `src/ledger.js` (cents, `sum`, `shownDiff`, `fSumE`, days to tenths below 10); views only format what it returns.
- Places: `data/places-<version>.json`, built by `npm run places` from GeoNames (ADR-0015), is fetched when an offer's form opens and kept in Cache Storage under its version; data saving or a 2G line defers it to the location field. `src/places.js` folds names and searches them; a municipality code is named through the list once it has arrived.
- Account sync keeps unsynced changes in `localStorage` (`cj.accountUnsynced`: changed and removed offer ids, settings), so offline edits survive a reload and are written first on the next connect; fingerprints are taken before each write. Signing out writes pending changes first, then clears the browser's copy, as does deleting the account (ADR-0017, ADR-0018).
- Shared rows: `ingestShared` fills the defaults; same-named sharers get a detail (place, then place or dates, then dates, then both, then «n.º N») from `sharedLabels` in `text.js`.
- The schema lives in `supabase/migrations/` (ADR-0016), applied in order by `test/unit/rls.test.js` and by `.github/workflows/db.yml`.

## Saved format and the version gate

- `src/schema.js` owns the saved format. `SCHEMA_VERSION` (5) is what is written to the account's `offers.schema_version` and to backups (`schema_version` beside `offers` and `cfg`). `upgrade[n]` turns version n into n + 1 (`offer` and `cfg`, each optional). Version 1: offers with every field at the top level, settings still holding former law figures. Version 2: `{ id, cmp, terms, mine }` and settings without them. Version 3: `offerOnly` and `place` in `terms` (ADR-0028; migration `20261104120000_offer_only_and_village_in_terms.sql`, `PAGE_VERSION` 2). Version 4: `contract: 'selfEmployed'` and `terms.vatIncluded` (ADR-0029; migration `20261105090000_self_employed.sql`, `PAGE_VERSION` 3). Version 5: `mine.record`, what happened (ADR-0032; migration `20261112120000_record_in_mine.sql`, `PAGE_VERSION` 4): `upgrade[4]` and the migration give every offer an empty `{ months: {}, payments: [] }`, and ingestion normalises it (`normalizeRecord`: unknown day kinds, non-finite amounts, unknown ways and malformed months are dropped, a payment without an id gets one, `DEF_MINE.record` is frozen and every offer takes its own copy). A shared offer has no `mine`, so it gets an empty record. `PAGE_VERSION` and `min_page_version` went up with it because a version-3 page holding an offer in memory (an unsynced edit kept at connect) would upsert `mine` without the record and erase what a newer page recorded; the account's stamp does not protect against it: an old page skips rows stamped above its schema, but its own unstamped in-memory copy is written whatever the row says. Fixture bundles per version are in `test/fixtures/schema/`, one test per upgrader in `test/unit/schema.test.js`.
- Every load path goes through it: `ingestSaved` (localStorage, backup file, account's own rows) and `ingestShared` (other shepherds' rows). An offer's version is its own `schema_version` (account rows), else its bundle's (backups), else its shape (browser copies were never stamped). Settings have no stamp in the account: they are treated as version 1, and their upgraders are idempotent.
- Writes are always the current version and keep fields the page does not know (`normalizeOffer` spreads them). A row of a newer version is read without its unknown fields, keeps its `schema_version` in memory and in the browser copy, and the account never writes it (`writable` in `storage/account.js`); a row of an older version is rewritten in the current one on connect.
- Browser copies have no stamp and read as version 2; `upgrade[2]` leaves an offer already in version 3 as it is, so that is harmless. For version 2, `mine.offerOnly` wins over a stale `terms.offerOnly` left from before ADR-0017.
- A new version needs: a batch migration that rewrites the rows and bumps `schema_version`, an `upgrade[n]` entry and fixture, and a higher `PAGE_VERSION` with `min_page_version` raised in the same migration when older pages could write the old shape.
- Version gate: `app_meta.min_page_version` (migration `20261104000000_min_page_version.sql`; a one-row table that anon and signed-in users can read and no API role can write) is the oldest page the database accepts. `PAGE_VERSION` in `schema.js`, raised by hand, is this page's. `storage/account.js` reads it on connect and before each flush that has something to write. A page below it stops syncing, reads nothing into the browser, sets the status `outdated` («Hay una versión nueva. Recarga la página.» on the account page and its button) and keeps recording its unsynced changes for after the reload (including the edits made before the account connected); signing out is refused while any are recorded, because it would clear them. A reconnect that finds the minimum lowered syncs again. A project without the `app_meta` table sets no minimum, so the page can ship before the migration is pushed.

## Self-employed (autónomo)

- Entered as the pay answer «Nada: se factura como autónomo» (`contract: 'selfEmployed'`); `payAmount` per `payUnit` is what is invoiced (per day: each day worked), with the IVA inside if `vatIncluded` (ADR-0029).
- `model/self-employed.js`: invoiced = agreed, less the IVA (10 %) if inside. Módulos income («rendimiento neto previo») = invoiced × 0,37. RETA tramo: that income less 7 % generic expenses, divided by the months registered (one without a duration); the quota is the tramo's minimum base × 31,50 % for each month. IRPF: on the módulos income less 5 %, the difference it makes to the year's tax on top of other offers' payslips (`irpfWithActivity`; above 6.500 € of activity income the earned-income reduction of those payslips goes, LIRPF art. 20). What is withheld on invoices (2 %) is shown, not counted.
- In `calc`, a self-employed offer has no payslip (like «sin contrato»), no employee's paro (the «cese de actividad» it contributes to is said, not calculated: at least 12 months contributed and a justified cessation), no severance, debts, savings or unpaid time; `parts.invoice` is what is invoiced and the quota and IRPF are taken off what is earned (`deductionsOf` in `units.js`, shared by the breakdown and `earnedShown`). `r.selfEmployed` holds every step.
- Limits: no en negro beside the invoice; other offers' IRPF ignores the autónomo's income; one activity; the quota at the tramo's minimum base; one tramo for a contract spanning two years.
