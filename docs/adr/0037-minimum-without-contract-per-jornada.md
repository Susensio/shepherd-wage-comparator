# ADR-0037: Measure a job without a contract of up to 120 days per jornada worked, and a longer one as fijo, instead of always as fijo

Status: Accepted (owner, 18 November 2026)
Date: 2026-11-18
Amends: ADR-0035 (its round-15 rule «no contract is measured as fijo, ET 15.4»)

## Context

Since round 15 every job without a contract was measured as fijo: the monthly minimum, vacation as a permanent job, the days worked instead of resting as «jornadas de más». An audit on 17 November 2026 found that ET 15.4 makes a worker without registration fijo only once a time equal to the trial period has passed, so the rule overreached on short jobs. The owner also found a vacation row on a seven-day cash job senseless, and asked how a temporero's minimum compares with a fijo's.

The two minimums, as the page states them:
- Per jornada (RD SMI art. 4, «eventuales y temporeros» of up to `casualMaxDays` = 120 days with one company): each day worked pays at least `casual` (57,82 € in 2026), which carries the share of Sundays, holidays and the two extra pays; the vacation share (30 days a year) is paid on top. The figure divides the yearly minimum over about 299 jornadas, a six-day week.
- Monthly (fijo): the yearly minimum (17.094 € in 2026) for the month, with 1,5 days of weekly rest (ET 37.1), holidays and 30 days of vacation, about 250–270 jornadas a year, so 62–68 € per day actually worked. A temporero working the same days earns 8–15 % less: the state's figure assumes one day of rest a week, not one and a half.
- For a short job worked every day both land close: Padarniú, 7 days in a row, 405 € per jornada against about 420–430 € as fijo with the days of rest worked as «jornadas de más».

Weighed and discarded:
- Always fijo (the round-15 rule): states more than ET 15.4 says for short jobs.
- Always per jornada: understates a long job without registration, which the law makes fijo.

## Decision

- A job without a contract of up to `casualMaxDays` (120) is measured per jornada worked: «cada día trabajado, al menos <casual> €». Longer, it is measured as fijo: the monthly minimum, with days worked instead of resting as «jornadas de más».
- Weekly rest is a right in both (ET 37.1; in agriculture it can be accumulated up to 14 days, RD 1561/1995); the per-jornada figure already pays the share of rest days, so a day worked instead of resting is paid as one more jornada, not twice.
- Without a contract, vacation is not asked and does not enter Corresponde, Cobrado or Falta (owner, 18 November 2026). Its right (the proportional share per jornada, or 30 days a year as fijo) is named once, in «¿Se cumplió la ley?» and in Vía legal, with its days and amount, not as a debt.
- The ficha says which measure applies in one line, e.g. «Sin contrato y hasta 120 días: cada día trabajado, al menos 57,82 € (Real Decreto del salario mínimo, art. 4)» or «Sin contrato más de 120 días: se mide como un trabajo fijo (Estatuto, art. 15.4)».

## Consequences

- The legal claim is never broader than the text it cites.
- Short cash jobs read simply: days worked times a figure, no vacation row.
- The 120-day boundary can move a job's minimum by a few euros; the ficha names the rule so the change is not silent.
- The per-jornada minimum is lower per worked day than a fijo's real one; the page states the law as it is, and the Leyes page explains the difference.
